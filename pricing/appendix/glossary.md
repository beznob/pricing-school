# Glossary — every term in one place, in plain words

Look things up here any time. Terms marked with a file number, like **(file 2.1)**, are taught properly
in that file. A short "advanced corner" at the bottom covers words you'll
meet in the repo's notebooks.

## A–C

- **AOP (Annual Operating Plan)** — the company's budget for the year: the
  revenue and profit you've promised, line by line. Pricing managers "own"
  the price-driven lines and must explain misses.
- **Basis point (bp)** — 0.01%. 100 bps = 1%. The native unit of FX and
  finance. ([File 1.2](../01_set_the_rate/02_percentages_and_basis_points.md))
- **Before/after comparison** — measuring a change by comparing the period
  before with the period after. Untrustworthy: everything else changed too.
  ([File 2.2](../02_prove_the_change/02_tests_and_experiments.md))
- **Break-even** — the sales level at which you stop losing money:
  fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit. ([File 1.1](../01_set_the_rate/01_price_cost_and_profit.md))
- **Buyback** — the shop buying your leftover holiday money back, at a rate
  worse again than the sell rate. ([File 1.2](../01_set_the_rate/02_percentages_and_basis_points.md))
- **Captive customer** — one who can't realistically go elsewhere (airport,
  stockout Friday). Fairness rules bite hardest here. ([File 4.1](../04_satisfy_the_regulator/01_segments_and_fairness.md))
- **Censored (data)** — a number that was cut off, not fully measured:
  sell-out day sales show the stock level, not the demand. ([File 2.4](../02_prove_the_change/04_forecasting_and_stockouts.md))
- **Click-and-collect (C&C)** — order online at the online rate, pick up in
  a branch.
- **Confidence interval** — the "range of likely values" around an estimate.
  Wide range = blurry measurement, *not* "no effect". ([File 2.1](../02_prove_the_change/01_averages_and_uncertainty.md))
- **Consumer Duty** — UK financial regulation (from the FCA) requiring fair
  outcomes for customers, including **fair value**: total price reasonable
  vs benefit, for every group. ([File 4.1](../04_satisfy_the_regulator/01_segments_and_fairness.md))
- **Contribution** — price minus variable cost; what one extra sale adds
  toward fixed costs and profit. ([File 1.1](../01_set_the_rate/01_price_cost_and_profit.md))
- **Control group** — the customers/branches deliberately left unchanged so
  you can see what would have happened anyway. ([File 2.2](../02_prove_the_change/02_tests_and_experiments.md))
- **Cross-subsidy** — one customer group's high prices funding another's low
  prices. Legal; must be checked for who carries it. ([File 4.1](../04_satisfy_the_regulator/01_segments_and_fairness.md))

## D–L

- **Difference-in-differences (DiD)** — (treated group's change) minus
  (control group's change): your effect with the market subtracted. ([File 2.2](../02_prove_the_change/02_tests_and_experiments.md))
- **Differential pricing** — charging different prices to different groups.
  Normal, useful, regulated. ([File 4.1](../04_satisfy_the_regulator/01_segments_and_fairness.md))
- **Elasticity** — "raise price 1%, lose how many % of volume?" Elastic
  means |number| > 1 (customers react strongly). ([File 1.3](../01_set_the_rate/03_elasticity.md))
- **Endogeneity** — when the price in your data was set *because of* demand
  (surging in peak season), so naive analysis of price vs volume is
  poisoned. ([File 1.3](../01_set_the_rate/03_elasticity.md) §6)
- **Expected value (EV)** — probability-weighted average of outcomes; the
  basic tool for deciding under uncertainty. ([File 2.1](../02_prove_the_change/01_averages_and_uncertainty.md))
- **Fair value** — see Consumer Duty. ([File 4.1](../04_satisfy_the_regulator/01_segments_and_fairness.md))
- **FCA** — the Financial Conduct Authority, the UK regulator of financial
  firms.
- **Fence** — a condition that hides a discount (order size, online-only),
  so price-sensitive customers get it without repricing everyone. ([File 1.5](../01_set_the_rate/05_competition.md))
- **Fixed cost** — paid regardless of sales volume (rent, salaries). ([File 1.1](../01_set_the_rate/01_price_cost_and_profit.md))
- **Forecast** — structured best guess of future demand from history.
  ([File 2.4](../02_prove_the_change/04_forecasting_and_stockouts.md))
- **Hedge** — a financial contract (e.g. a *forward*: agreeing today's rate
  for a future purchase) that protects your inventory's value from market
  moves. "70% hedge cover" = 70% of the exposure is protected.
- **Holdout** — a group deliberately kept on the old price/plan after
  rollout, to keep measuring the true effect. ([File 2.2](../02_prove_the_change/02_tests_and_experiments.md))
- **Implicit margin** — profit hidden inside a rate rather than shown as a
  fee ("0% commission"). ([File 1.2](../01_set_the_rate/02_percentages_and_basis_points.md))
- **Interbank / mid-market / wholesale rate** — the fair no-profit exchange
  rate banks trade at; the benchmark all spreads are measured from. ([File 1.2](../01_set_the_rate/02_percentages_and_basis_points.md))
- **Leakage** — the gap between the price you set and the price you actually
  collect, from unmanaged discounts and errors. ([File 1.4](../01_set_the_rate/04_price_volume_mix_and_leakage.md))
- **Lerner rule** — profit-maximising margin ≈ 1 ÷ |elasticity|. Only
  meaningful for elastic customers. ([File 1.3](../01_set_the_rate/03_elasticity.md))
- **List price** — the official price on the rate board, before any
  discounts. ([File 1.4](../01_set_the_rate/04_price_volume_mix_and_leakage.md))

## M–R

- **MAG (Minimum Annual Guarantee)** — in an airport concession contract,
  rent you must pay *even if sales collapse*. Makes falling footfall doubly
  painful.
- **Margin** — (price − cost) ÷ price. Compare **markup**: (price − cost) ÷
  cost. ([File 1.1](../01_set_the_rate/01_price_cost_and_profit.md))
- **MDE (minimum detectable effect)** — the smallest effect a test could
  reliably see. Ask for it *before* testing. ([File 2.2](../02_prove_the_change/02_tests_and_experiments.md))
- **Mean / median** — everyday average / the middle value. Median describes
  the typical customer; means get dragged by whales. ([File 2.1](../02_prove_the_change/01_averages_and_uncertainty.md))
- **Mix effect** — profit (or fairness) change caused by the *blend* of what
  sold shifting, with prices and volume unchanged. ([File 1.4](../01_set_the_rate/04_price_volume_mix_and_leakage.md) and [file 4.1](../04_satisfy_the_regulator/01_segments_and_fairness.md))
- **Pocket price** — what actually lands in your pocket after all
  discounts and leaks. ([File 1.4](../01_set_the_rate/04_price_volume_mix_and_leakage.md))
- **Power (of a test)** — a test's ability to see an effect of a given size.
  Low power → "no significant result" is expected even when the effect is
  real. ([File 2.1](../02_prove_the_change/01_averages_and_uncertainty.md) and [file 2.2](../02_prove_the_change/02_tests_and_experiments.md))
- **Price walking** — quietly raising prices on loyal renewing customers vs
  new ones. Banned in UK insurance since 2022; a useful cautionary tale.
- **Price war** — competitors cutting in response to each other until
  everyone earns less. See prisoner's dilemma. ([File 1.5](../01_set_the_rate/05_competition.md))
- **Prisoner's dilemma** — the trap where each side's individually smart
  move (cut price) leaves both worse off. ([File 1.5](../01_set_the_rate/05_competition.md))
- **Promotional vs structural** — a competitor move that will end (funded
  splash) vs one that won't (lower-cost rival). Diagnose before responding.
  ([File 1.5](../01_set_the_rate/05_competition.md))
- **Randomisation** — assigning who gets the change by random draw, making
  the groups genuinely comparable. The guarantee behind a fair test. ([File 2.2](../02_prove_the_change/02_tests_and_experiments.md))
- **Revenue** — price × volume. Money in, before any costs. ([File 1.1](../01_set_the_rate/01_price_cost_and_profit.md))

## S–Z

- **Seasonality** — repeating demand patterns (Fridays, summer). ([File 2.4](../02_prove_the_change/04_forecasting_and_stockouts.md))
- **Segment** — a group of customers who behave similarly. ([File 4.1](../04_satisfy_the_regulator/01_segments_and_fairness.md))
- **Spillover / interference** — a test leaking between groups (customers
  walking from a control branch to a treated one), corrupting the
  comparison. ([File 2.2](../02_prove_the_change/02_tests_and_experiments.md))
- **Spread** — the gap between the interbank rate and the customer's rate;
  where FX profit lives. ([File 1.2](../01_set_the_rate/02_percentages_and_basis_points.md))
- **Standard deviation** — how far from the average a typical observation
  sits; the wobble. ([File 2.1](../02_prove_the_change/01_averages_and_uncertainty.md))
- **Stockout** — running out of stock while customers still want to buy;
  turns your sales data into a lie. ([File 2.4](../02_prove_the_change/04_forecasting_and_stockouts.md))
- **Tier** — a better price at a size threshold (£500+, £1,000+). ([File 1.2](../01_set_the_rate/02_percentages_and_basis_points.md))
- **TMC (Travel Money Card)** — at First Rate the Post Office Travel Money Card, the prepaid multi-currency Mastercard the company issues. In the role profile TMC is that product, whose P&L (new card sales, reloads, in-life revenue) the role owns. It is not a travel management company. See file 0.4.
- **Travel Management Company** — a business-travel agency; a
  business-to-business sales channel with contracted, inelastic pricing.
- **Variable cost** — paid per sale (the goods themselves, card fees).
  ([File 1.1](../01_set_the_rate/01_price_cost_and_profit.md))
- **Vulnerable customer** — a customer less able to protect their own
  interests (health, financial resilience, digital skills, life events);
  the group the Consumer Duty requires you to check outcomes for. ([File 4.1](../04_satisfy_the_regulator/01_segments_and_fairness.md))
- **Waterfall (price waterfall)** — the step-by-step chart from list price
  down every giveaway to pocket price. ([File 1.4](../01_set_the_rate/04_price_volume_mix_and_leakage.md))

## Toolkit terms — from the appendix and stage 3

- **AIC** — a score for comparing fitted models/distributions: lower is
  better; it rewards fit and punishes complexity. ([The distributions appendix](distributions_for_pricing.md))
- **AUC / Gini** — how well a model separates buyers from non-buyers.
  AUC 0.5 = coin flip, 1.0 = perfect; Gini = 2×AUC − 1. ([The workflow appendix](data_science_workflow.md))
- **Capping** — limiting extreme values: *feature* capping protects the
  model from outliers; *price* capping protects customers (and you) from
  extreme quotes and jumps. ([The workflow appendix](data_science_workflow.md))
- **Combined ratio** — (claims + expenses) ÷ premium earned. Below 100% =
  underwriting profit. Insurance's favourite KPI. ([File 3.2](../03_defend_the_plan/02_kpis_and_stakeholders.md))
- **Compound distribution / frequency × severity** — total cost = a random
  *count* of events × random *sizes*; expected total = E[count] × E[size].
  ([The distributions appendix](distributions_for_pricing.md))
- **Conversion rate** — quotes that become sales; the direct read on price
  competitiveness (comparison-site feel: ~4–8% healthy). ([The workflow appendix](data_science_workflow.md) and [file 3.2](../03_defend_the_plan/02_kpis_and_stakeholders.md))
- **CLV (customer lifetime value)** — total expected profit over the whole
  customer relationship. ([File 3.2](../03_defend_the_plan/02_kpis_and_stakeholders.md))
- **GLM (generalised linear model)** — a regression where you choose the
  output's shape (yes/no, count, positive amount); the industry's auditable
  standard. ([The workflow appendix](data_science_workflow.md))
- **GWP (gross written premium)** — insurance's top-line revenue. ([File 3.2](../03_defend_the_plan/02_kpis_and_stakeholders.md))
- **Log-normal / Poisson / negative binomial / gamma / Pareto** — the money
  shape, the counting shape, the clumpy-counting shape, the cost shape, the
  monster-tail shape. ([The distributions appendix](distributions_for_pricing.md))
- **Out-of-time (OOT) validation** — testing a model on the most *recent*
  period rather than a random shuffle; the validation pricing trusts.
  ([The workflow appendix](data_science_workflow.md))
- **Overdispersion** — counts whose variance exceeds their mean (events
  cluster); the sign to switch Poisson → negative binomial. ([The distributions appendix](distributions_for_pricing.md))
- **Overfitting** — a model memorising its training data's noise; brilliant
  in the lab, wrong live. ([The workflow appendix](data_science_workflow.md))
- **PSI (population stability index)** — a drift score comparing today's
  data mix to the training mix. <0.1 fine · 0.1–0.25 watch · >0.25
  investigate. ([The workflow appendix](data_science_workflow.md))
- **Revenue bridge** — the volume/price/mix/competitor decomposition that
  explains plan-vs-actual, line by line. ([File 1.4](../01_set_the_rate/04_price_volume_mix_and_leakage.md) and [file 3.2](../03_defend_the_plan/02_kpis_and_stakeholders.md))
- **SHAP** — per-prediction explanations with direction ("this feature
  pushed the price up"); the tool for convincing humans and regulators.
  ([The workflow appendix](data_science_workflow.md))

## Formula cheat-sheet

| Quantity | Formula | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Own-price elasticity | `ε = %ΔQ / %ΔP` | Elastic if \|ε\| > 1 ([File 1.3](../01_set_the_rate/03_elasticity.md)) |
| Arc elasticity | `[(Q₁−Q₀)/avg Q] / [(P₁−P₀)/avg P]` | Symmetric to direction |
| Cross-price elasticity | `ε_xy = %ΔQ_x / %ΔP_y` | +ve = substitutes ([File 1.7](../01_set_the_rate/07_channels_promotions_revenue_management.md)) |
| Constant-elasticity demand | `Q = A·P^ε` → `log Q = a + ε·log P` | The log-log workhorse ([file 2.3](../02_prove_the_change/03_causal_inference_primer.md) §12) |
| Marginal revenue | `MR = P(1 − 1/\|ε\|)` | Profit-max: MR = MC |
| Lerner rule | `(P − c)/P = 1/\|ε\|` → `P* = c\|ε\|/(\|ε\|−1)` | Needs \|ε\| > 1 ([File 1.3](../01_set_the_rate/03_elasticity.md) and [file 1.7](../01_set_the_rate/07_channels_promotions_revenue_management.md)) |
| Revenue-max point | ε = −1 | Profit-max is more inelastic |
| Contribution margin ratio | `(P − V)/P` | ([File 1.1](../01_set_the_rate/01_price_cost_and_profit.md)) |
| Break-even volume | `fixed cost / (P − V)` | ([File 1.1](../01_set_the_rate/01_price_cost_and_profit.md)) |
| Discount break-even uplift | `d / (CM − d)` | Volume needed after a cut d ([File 1.7](../01_set_the_rate/07_channels_promotions_revenue_management.md)) |
| Price-rise volume tolerance | `d / (CM + d)` | Volume you can lose after a rise d |
| Elasticity-implied volume | `(1 − d)^ε − 1` | Expected volume from a cut d |
| LTV (steady state) | `ARPU × CM / churn`; discounted: `Σ margin_t/(1+r)^t` | ([File 3.3](../03_defend_the_plan/03_operating_model_and_governance.md)) |
| LTV / CAC | ≈ 3× healthy rule of thumb | Pair with payback |
| Conjoint WTP | `−(β_attr / β_price)` | Utility → £ ([File 1.6](../01_set_the_rate/06_strategy_and_offer_design.md)) |
| Price realisation | `pocket / list` | Leakage ([File 3.3](../03_defend_the_plan/03_operating_model_and_governance.md)) |
| PSI (drift) | `Σ (a% − e%)·ln(a%/e%)` | >0.25 = significant shift ([The workflow appendix](data_science_workflow.md)) |
| First-stage F | `(coef/se)²` for a single IV | >10 = strong instrument ([file 2.3](../02_prove_the_change/03_causal_inference_primer.md) §12) |
| NRR | `(start + expansion − contraction − churn) / start` | >100% = net growth ([File 1.6](../01_set_the_rate/06_strategy_and_offer_design.md)) |
| Expected total (freq × sev) | `E[S] = E[N] × E[X]` | ([The distributions appendix](distributions_for_pricing.md)) |

## Advanced corner — words from the repo's notebooks

One-line versions; the notebooks in [`../05_the_panel/README.md`](../05_the_panel/README.md)
teach them properly.

- **Bandit (multi-armed bandit)** — an algorithm that learns the best price
  by trying options and keeping what works, balancing *exploring* (trying)
  against *exploiting* (using the current best).
- **CUPED** — the "compare each branch to its own baseline" trick that makes
  tests sharper for free. ([File 2.2](../02_prove_the_change/02_tests_and_experiments.md) §4, idea 2)
- **DML (double machine learning)** — a method for estimating how elasticity
  varies customer-by-customer when many factors move at once.
- **Instrumental variable (IV)** — a way to rescue elasticity from
  endogeneity: find something that moves *price* but has nothing to do with
  *demand* (e.g. the wholesale cost), and measure through it.
- **Off-policy evaluation (OPE)** — scoring a pricing rule from logged data
  *without* ever running it on live customers.
- **Synthetic control** — building a "twin" for something you couldn't
  randomise (a competitor opening) from a weighted blend of unaffected
  branches, then reading the effect off the gap. ([File 1.5](../01_set_the_rate/05_competition.md) §5 is the simple
  version)
- **Thompson sampling** — a well-behaved bandit that explores in proportion
  to its own uncertainty.
