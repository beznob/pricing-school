## The role in one paragraph

First Rate Exchange Services, the 50/50 joint venture of Post Office and Bank of
Ireland, sells travel money through the Post Office branch network, online for home
delivery and branch collection, and through wholesale and agency partners, and it
issues the Post Office Travel Money Card, a prepaid multi-currency Mastercard. The
price is the FX margin, the gap between the wholesale rate and the rate on the
board, plus the card's fees. This role reports to the Chief Revenue Officer, manages
one person, and owns that margin across every channel, runs the tests that justify
moving it, builds the revenue lines into the annual operating plan, evidences to the
FCA that customers got fair value, owns the card's P&L, and leads the commercial
side of the card's migration to a new processor. It is a commercial role that needs
real statistics and a project it has to see through.

## One reading to get right

In the role profile, **TMC means the Travel Money Card**, the prepaid product. "Own
the P&L for TMC, including new card sales, reloads and in-life revenue" is a card
P&L. It does not mean a travel management company.

## What makes it a hard pricing problem

Three things, and they run through every stage that follows.

The Post Office is both a shareholder and the channel whose postmasters are paid a
share of the income, and from April 2026 that share rose to at least 55% on travel
products with £5 per card sold, so a decision that improves group profit can still
hurt one side of the partnership. Walk-in cash cannot be randomised at customer
level, so the evidence has to come from regional tests rather than clean A/B splits.
And the price is quoted as a rate rather than a fee, which means most customers
cannot easily compare it, so fair value is a live regulatory question rather than a
box to tick.
